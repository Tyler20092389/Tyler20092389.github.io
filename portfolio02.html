UTC OLP Student

In my T-Level placement, I spent 45 days working on a part of a project at university with four other students from UTC. The project was to work together to make a robot that helps patients with their physio, regaining movement in their limbs and keeping track of their progress without breaking patient confidentiality. There were four parts to this project: The eyes of the robot to actually see the progress and the patient, the UI designs, the neuromorphic computing for interactive robots, and the movement of the actual robot itself using motion retargeting which was my part. I did everything in Visual Studio Code, also using the built-in AI to help.

My part of the project was to track the movement of the human, then relay that to the robot, so that it can track the progress of the person’s movements and copy their movements to show them how to improve. I started this off by getting a stick figure to move in a BVH player, so I know could at least be able to get some movement. Once I got this working, I then went onto making a stick figure that stood still, this would be the resting position of the figure. I then added a camera window so that the camera could see the person’s movements, and beside it was the stick figure that mirrored the person’s movements. I used pooling (taking away extra limbs and joints) to get rid of any glitches as having more connections makes the movement more sensitive to follow. I got it so that it could mirror basic movement, I got this part working.

My next step would’ve been to use skeletal convolution (opposite of pooling, adding more joints and limbs instead of removing) to give a more realistic outcome through the stick figure, down to the point where if the patient moved their finger, so would the figure. This would then convey to the robot to move in that way, allowing it to copy the patient’s movements then compare it to the way a normal person would move, therefore being able to tell where that patient needs to improve their movements.

Difficult: Understanding properly what I was being asked to do at the beginning.

Success: Managed to get some movement and detection working.
